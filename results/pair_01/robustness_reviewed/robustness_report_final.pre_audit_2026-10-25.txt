6. METHODOLOGICAL LIMITATION REGARDING ISOLATED REGISTRATION TRENDS
-------------------------------------------------------------------
CRITICAL SCIENTIFIC NOTE:
The isolated translation and rotation experiments utilize fixed perturbation directions and rotation
axes (deterministic seed = 42). The observed metric curves describe the response of C2C under these
specific rigid transformations on the Bike Parking Construction site geometry.

DO NOT INFER THAT SMALL REGISTRATION ERRORS GENERALLY IMPROVE DETECTION:
The slight apparent rise in Macro F1 at small perturbations (e.g. from 0.7359 at baseline to 0.7568 at
5 cm translation, or to 0.7677 at 0.50° rotation) is an artifact of this specific transformation vector
relative to the local site layout:
  1. In the clean reference scan, a subset of newly added construction elements is situated very close
     (within 7.5 cm) to preexisting unchanged geometry. C2C cannot detect changes closer than tau,
     resulting in missed detections (false negatives).
  2. The specific translation vector and centroid rotation happened to shift newly added elements away
     from adjacent static walls, artificially pushing them across the 7.5 cm threshold and increasing
     Scan 1 recall (from 75.3% to 85.1%).
  3. A translation in the opposite direction, or along a surface normal, would have immediately inflated
     false alarms or suppressed recall.

In operational construction monitoring, registration errors are unknown and variable. Small registration
errors never improve real-world reliability; strict co-registration remains essential.

11. OVERALL SYNTHESIS & MOTIVATION FOR STAGE 4
----------------------------------------------
This finalized evaluation rigorously isolates each physical failure mode of standard Euclidean C2C:
  1. Rigid translation above 7.5 cm induces catastrophic false positives.
  2. Rigid rotation causes peripheral degradation proportional to radius.
  3. Occlusion generates severe phantom change alarms unless visibility-aware filtering is applied.
  4. High noise degrades precision along surface boundaries.

These empirical findings provide the solid quantitative benchmark against which M3C2 (Stage 4) and
deep learning change detectors (Stage 5) will be measured.
