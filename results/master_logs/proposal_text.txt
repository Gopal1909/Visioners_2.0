Why comparing two scans is hard
Same wall, scanned twice. Grey is scan 1, orange is scan 2.
Registration error
The scans are not perfectly 
aligned, so a wall that did not 
move looks shifted.
Sensor noise
Points scatter around the true 
surface.
Occlusion
Something blocks the view, so 
part of the wall is missing in 
one scan.
Different density
One scan has far fewer points 
than the other.
A plain scan-to-scan comparison marks all of this as change. The real construction changes get buried in 
false alarms.
What we want to do
Objective
Build a 3D change-detection pipeline that finds 
added, removed, or unchanged regions between 
two scans between two LiDAR scans of the same 
site, and check how well it works when the scans 
are imperfect.
What is new
We are not proposing another model. The published 
benchmark studies how methods generalise across 
scenes. We study how they hold up as registration 
error, sparsity, noise and occlusion increase.

The datasets
• MultiChange3D (ETH Zurich and FBK): a 
benchmark for 3D geometric change detection, 
published at ISPRS 2026
• We use its Bike parking construction scene: 
MLS, 4 epochs, 6 epoch pairs, about 5 million 
points
• Every point is labelled unchanged, added or 
removed
• ConSLAM: real construction site scanned 
monthly by a Cambridge team, five sequences, 
TLS ground truth
• MultiChange3D → quantitative evaluation
ConSLAM → qualitative real-site validation
